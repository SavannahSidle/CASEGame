This checklist captures the coordinated art direction used for the separate standing-portrait pass: believable anatomy, a natural face, controlled hair volume, fine hands, readable fabric, and detailed mukluks. S's approved portrait remains preserved in the roster.

Silhouette, anatomy, and balance

  1. Set one consistent adult height and head-to-body scale across the whole figure.
  2. Align the head, sternum, and pelvis along a believable weight line.
  3. Give the neck a natural, gently angled connection to the posed head.
  4. Introduce slight, natural shoulder asymmetry instead of a mirrored mannequin line.
  5. Shape both clavicles with a smooth curve from the sternum toward the shoulder.
  6. Shorten the viewer-left clavicle so it does not run unnaturally into the shoulder.
  7. Remove the small raised bumps along the opposite clavicle.
  8. Define the shoulder caps softly without flattening their underlying anatomy.
  9. Reduce visible muscle striation in the arms while preserving believable volume.
  10. Clarify the elbow landmarks without making them sharp or bony.
  11. Taper both forearms gradually toward the wrists.
  12. Match each wrist's scale to its palm and forearm.
  13. Balance ribcage width against the pelvis from this camera angle.
  14. Give the waist a modestly fuller, more natural circumference.
  15. Avoid a pinched hourglass transition at the narrowest point of the waist.
  16. Blend ribcage, waist, and pelvis with continuous anatomical planes.
  17. Keep shoulder and hip angles consistent with the three-quarter perspective.
  18. Place the body's center of gravity over the supporting foot.
  19. Keep the knees and ankles aligned with the actual stance.
  20. Let each foot sit at a slightly different angle to support a relaxed pose.
