The 200-point checklist is split into 100 hairline/surrounding-area checks and 100 leg/legging checks.

Visible S++ art pass

  • Kept the viewer-left swept hair loop, reduced crown bulk, and refined the front hairline and root flow.
  • Kept blue eyes and light, fine brows; retained the visible two-lobe earring pair with lower star and upper purple gem.

1–10. Forehead arc and skull fit

  1. Map the hair edge to the forehead's curved bony plane instead of drawing a straight cap-like line.
  2. Lower the frontal edge slightly so the forehead does not read unusually tall.
  3. Shift the most prominent front edge toward the viewer-right as requested, while keeping the face recognizable.
  4. Use a gentle convex arc across the center forehead that rolls naturally into both temples.
  5. Allow subtle left-right asymmetry so the hairline does not look stamped from a template.
  6. Keep the center-front contour soft rather than creating a sharp triangular peak.
  7. Blend the edge into the crown according to the skull's turn in three-quarter view.
  8. Let the hairline recede gradually at the temples instead of ending abruptly above the ears.
  9. Keep the hairline's height consistent with brow, glabella, and forehead landmarks.
  10. Check the silhouette at thumbnail size for a natural scalp-to-forehead transition.

11–20. Part, scalp, and root direction

  1. Place the part where the head's crown and swept style can plausibly generate the visible flow.
  2. Use an irregular narrow scalp part rather than a broad stripe of exposed scalp.
  3. Carry the part through the correct perspective plane instead of drawing it as a flat line.
  4. Show a subtle change in root direction on either side of the part.
  5. Keep the scalp visible only where the hair separates under tension.
  6. Blend root color into the lengths without a harsh band or painted-on highlight.
  7. Anchor each visible lock to a believable root area.
  8. Make root density slightly tighter than the loose mid-lengths.
  9. Let the roots follow the skull rather than floating above it.
  10. Keep part edges softly broken by a few fine strands.

21–30. Hairline microstructure and baby hairs

  1. Break the perimeter into fine tapered hairs rather than a hard digital outline.
  2. Vary baby-hair length at the front edge with a restrained, natural rhythm.
  3. Keep fine forehead hairs sparse enough to preserve a clean facial read.
  4. Taper each visible baby hair toward its tip instead of ending bluntly.
  5. Vary the baby hairs' direction to follow local growth and styling tension.
  6. Use tiny gaps between selected wisps so the edge breathes against the skin.
  7. Keep short hairs close to the scalp instead of turning them into long curls.
  8. Softly integrate peach-fuzz-like edge hairs with the skin's lighting.
  9. Avoid repeating the same scallop pattern along the entire hairline.
  10. Ensure wisps do not overlap eyebrows, eyes, or the defining front loop.

31–40. Temples and sideburn transitions

  1. Shape the temple notch to suit the forehead width and brow position.
  2. Blend temple density gradually into the side hair rather than showing a bald wedge.
  3. Let the sideburn taper toward the ear with a soft, believable edge.
  4. Keep the hair in front of the ear fine enough to reveal the ear silhouette.
  5. Preserve the visible two-lobe-earring area without stray strands covering the studs.
  6. Let temple locks follow the cheek plane without sticking straight outward.
  7. Use a few shorter face-framing strands to soften the temple transition.
  8. Keep left and right temple shapes related but not mechanically identical.
  9. Separate the temple shadow from the hair mass with soft value changes.
  10. Check that the temple edge remains plausible when the head turns.

41–50. Root flow and lock construction

  1. Organize hair into primary, secondary, and fine strand layers.
  2. Keep major locks broad enough to read as hair masses at gameplay scale.
  3. Use finer strands only to explain edges, overlaps, and direction changes.
  4. Vary lock widths so every strand does not have identical thickness.
  5. Let roots emerge in fan-like groups that merge into larger locks.
  6. Show overlapping locks with clear front-to-back order.
  7. Keep individual strand highlights aligned with the lock's curvature.
  8. Make strand ends taper and separate without multiplying into noisy threads.
  9. Use gentle changes in curvature through the mid-lengths instead of parallel tubes.
  10. Make each visible lock connect to the hairstyle's overall sweep.

51–60. Crown height and volume control

  1. Flatten the crown to follow the skull rather than forming a raised cap.
  2. Reduce far-side crown volume so it does not overpower the face in profile.
  3. Keep lift concentrated only where the hairstyle's sweep requires it.
  4. Blend top strands into the crown without a separate puffed mound.
  5. Use shallow layered overlap to suggest volume without increasing the silhouette.
  6. Preserve the natural head shape beneath the hair mass.
  7. Keep crown highlights broad and soft rather than creating bead-like ridges.
  8. Let the crown slope continuously toward the back of the head.
  9. Break the top outline with only a few fine flyaways.
  10. Compare the crown silhouette with the brow-to-chin scale to avoid a top-heavy head.

61–70. Signature swept-over loop

  1. Keep the distinctive loop on the viewer-left as a clear, readable signature feature.
  2. Make the loop's root visibly connect to the main sweep instead of appearing attached.
  3. Give the loop an even, controlled arc without a kink at its apex.
  4. Maintain a believable strand bundle inside the loop rather than an empty ring.
  5. Keep the loop's opening clean enough to read against the background.
  6. Use a small range of strand widths through the loop to imply layered hair.
  7. Make the loop lie close to the scalp where the style pulls it back.
  8. Allow subtle overlap where the loop crosses the crown.
  9. Preserve the loop's height while reducing unrelated crown puffiness.
  10. Ensure the loop stays distinct from loose flyaways and side strands.

71–80. Face-framing locks and silhouette

  1. Use face-framing strands to clarify the forehead-to-temple transition.
  2. Keep front locks clear of the eye and eyebrow silhouette.
  3. Let cheek-side strands follow the cheek's contour without hiding the jaw.
  4. Vary the gaps between locks so the face does not sit behind a solid hair curtain.
  5. Keep the far-side outline narrower in keeping with three-quarter perspective.
  6. Let back lengths layer behind the shoulder and arm in a readable order.
  7. Shape the outer silhouette with a mix of long curves and restrained breaks.
  8. Use tapered ends around the shoulder instead of a single blunt shelf.
  9. Keep hair-to-skin occlusion clean at the neck and ear.
  10. Check that no stray edge reads as an extra loop or separate object.

81–90. Color, sheen, and strand texture

  1. Use a natural brunette base with small warm-cool shifts rather than a single brown fill.
  2. Keep root shadows deeper but soft enough to preserve strand detail.
  3. Place highlights along the curve of each lock, not as straight painted stripes.
  4. Vary highlight width with strand grouping and light direction.
  5. Retain midtone detail between highlights so the hair does not look metallic.
  6. Use subtle hue variation through the lengths without changing the overall brunette color.
  7. Keep fine flyaway highlights low contrast at normal game size.
  8. Reserve the brightest accents for the top-facing curves.
  9. Separate overlapping locks with value changes instead of black outline strokes.
  10. Prevent repeating texture motifs that make the hair look tiled.

  9. Check the hairstyle at full resolution and at small gameplay scale.
