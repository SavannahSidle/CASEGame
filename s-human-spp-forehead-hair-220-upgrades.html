Forehead and hair refinement

A close-up review checklist for the updated S++ portrait. The portrait applies the requested natural curved hairline, flatter crown, loose unbraided strands, finer facial proportions, fuller mid-torso, chest paw prints, and bare feet. These notes define the forehead and strand-level hair targets for close-up review and future strand animation.

Applied in the portrait

  • Eyes aligned into a natural side gaze; nose tip narrowed and tapered; upper lip slimmed with a slight smile.
  • Forehead and hairline shaped as a curved, lower transition; crown volume reduced; signature viewer-left sweep preserved.
  • Hair rendered as flowing, separate loose locks with fine strands and wisps, without braids or woven sections.
  • Waist made fuller, two separated paw-print tattoos added below the collarbones, and boots removed for bare feet.

20 forehead refinements and 200 strand-level hair refinements, organized for review. The hair points specify texture, grouping, shading, silhouette, depth, and rigging priorities; the portrait remains a raster asset rather than a 200-control hair rig.

Forehead · 20 improvements

Forehead structure and surface

  1. Shape the forehead as one softly convex plane from the curved hairline to the brow ridge.
  2. Follow the skull's front-to-side turn with a gradual transition rather than a flat panel.
  3. Place a broad, soft highlight along the central plane, following the light direction.
  4. Blend both temple planes with diffuse shadow instead of sharp dark edges.
  5. Remove any dark stripe or pinched shadow between the brows; keep the glabella relaxed.
  6. Keep the brow ridge subtle and adult without a heavy shelf.
  7. Balance the two sides while preserving slight natural asymmetry.
  8. Keep forehead height proportional to the eyes, nose, and hairline.
  9. Use fine, low-contrast skin texture that holds up at close zoom.
  10. Shift skin tone gently across center, temples, and hairline without visible bands.
  11. Keep the highlight broad and soft so the forehead does not look plastic.
  12. Fade the forehead shading toward the nose bridge instead of making a hard crease.
  13. Shape the temples so they narrow gradually toward the outer brow.
  14. Use a curved, irregular hairline rather than a long straight edge.
  15. Add only a few fine baby hairs along the hairline, not a uniform fringe.
  16. Keep tiny asymmetries so the contour looks naturally grown.
  17. Show roots without painting a dark outline across the skin.
  18. Preserve the relaxed expression through the brow-to-forehead transition.
  19. Avoid noisy speckles, over-sharpened pores, or repeated texture stamps.
  20. Check the forehead at thumbnail, normal, and close-up sizes.
